Student guide

From a water budget to a recommendation

Purpose: Interpret an annual water budget, test a management choice, explain a groundwater–stream connection, and evaluate who might benefit or bear a burden. Plan for approximately 35–50 minutes after the water-resources readings.

1. Establish the baselineWith no facility, no added conservation, and no added pumping, record one trial. Explain whether drought creates a shortage before the facility exists.
2. Investigate and reviseCompare both facility designs. Record a first plan, then change one decision control and record a revision. Explain why that change matters.
3. Challenge your planTest your preferred plan at 50% conservation achievement. Examine both climates. If your conservation target is zero, explain why the fraction has no effect, then test a nonzero target. Use evidence to recommend approval, modification, deferral, or rejection.

Submit in Canvas

Submit your notebook containing at least three recorded trials, plus a 250–350 word recommendation or a 2–3 minute audio/video explanation with the same evidence. Upload the files to the Canvas assignment; this site does not grade or submit your work.

  1. State your recommendation and use at least two numerical results with climate labels and units.
  2. Explain a possible effect of added pumping on shallow wells or the connected stream, including a scientific mechanism.
  3. Compare two community perspectives. Identify a possible benefit or burden of your plan without inventing neighborhood outcomes.
  4. Explain the 50% test result, one model limitation, and one specific measurement or implementation fact that could change your recommendation.
Independent reasoning

Use the activity, course readings, calculators, and instructor support. Do not use generative AI to run your investigation, interpret results, or draft/revise your submission. Follow approved accessibility accommodations.

Critical Lens: who can conserve, and who can absorb risk?

Household water access: Some residents use shallow wells. What evidence would reveal whether a pumping proposal places them at additional risk?

Agricultural livelihoods: A new conservation target needs an implementation plan. Which practices, costs, and participation barriers would you investigate?

Stream and habitat: The annual balance does not calculate streamflow. What monitoring would help test a prediction about pumping and stream conditions?

Development opportunities: Job and tax-revenue estimates remain unverified. What evidence would you request before weighing those benefits?
